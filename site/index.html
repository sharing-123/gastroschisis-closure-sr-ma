Closing gastroschisis, what the evidence says

A systematic review and meta-analysis of silo-based delayed closure, sutureless closure and fascial closure in neonates. 42 studies, 35 pooled, about 5,800 infants.

Fig. 1

Three ways to close one defect

  1. FC

    Fascial closure

    The surgeon reduces the bowel and sutures the fascia at the first operation.

  2. DC

    Delayed closure after a silo

    The bowel returns in stages inside a silo, and the defect is closed days later.

  3. SL

    Sutureless closure

    The defect closes around the umbilical cord or a dressing, with no fascial sutures.

Infants with swollen or damaged bowel are more likely to receive a silo, and they stay longer for that reason. A raw comparison mixes the technique with the bowel. The review therefore reports prediction intervals and grades the certainty of every estimate.

Seven further reports gave only adjusted associations, medians without spread or a before-and-after comparison, and are synthesised narratively. Ten journals were hand-searched. Search date 22 November 2026.

How much to trust it

Risk of bias was serious in 31 of 32 non-randomized studies, mostly from confounding by indication. Certainty is therefore low or very low for every outcome.

Fig. 4

At the bedside

Silo-based delayed closure

A silo goes with a longer stay, more ventilator days and more parenteral nutrition than fascial closure. Confounding by indication limits a causal reading, so reserve the silo for infants who cannot tolerate primary reduction and do not choose it to improve outcomes.

Sutureless closure

In simple gastroschisis, sutureless closure shortens ventilation by about 1.5 days and leaves the stay unchanged. Hernia risk is unresolved (RR 3.11, 95% CI 0.34 to 28.18, two studies).

What would settle it

Trials, or adjusted patient-level analyses, stratified by bowel condition. The three randomized trials so far are small and one stopped early.

Methods

Open data, open code

Pairwise random-effects meta-analysis with network and multilevel models as checks. Every estimate on this page comes from the files in the repository.
